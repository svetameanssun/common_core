<h1 align="left">42 Programming School Common Core Projects</h1>

If you have never heard of **42 Programming School**, you might wonder what differentiates it from a myriad of other courses you may find online. The answer to this shrewd question might be summarized in one word:

## **THE METHODOLOGY**

(Well, there are technically two words, but they represent a single concept.)

The 42 methodology consists of throwing a project at students and having them try to finish it before the deadline.

**No teachers**, **no textbooks**.

You can find help (and some emotional support) only among fellow students: those who have already completed the project or those who have just started it, just like you.
And also those who are in the middle of doing it. Or those who have never done it but have some idea about the underlying technology... I mean, literally any other student!

You end up learning complex new concepts through trial and error and constant discussions with like-minded people.

Doesn't it sound like a real-world work experience?
## **What is the COMMON CORE?**

The Common Core is a set of projects that helps you establish a solid foundation to start your journey as a software developer
(or practically any other IT role).

The range of projects spans from writing standard **C library** functions from scratch (`strlen`, `memcmp`, `calloc`, etc.) and recreating your own shell in **C**, to broadening your system administration knowledge using **Docker** and creating your own **IRC** (Internet Relay Chat) server.

You dive into every project understanding that if you do not meet the set deadline, you are out!
  
So, 42 not only gives you the opportunity to work on projects to improve your understanding of the core programming concepts; it also teaches you to handle high pressure when acquiring new knowledge, helps you improve your time management, and forces you to meet deadlines (the latter three points are the most valuable skills you can gain, in my personal opinion).

*Feel free to check out my repositories to see some of the Common Core projects I've finished.*

## **What happens after the COMMON CORE?**
Some students start looking for a job while working on Common Core projects, and begin working right after completing them. Others choose to pursue the 42 Paris's accredited RNCP Level 6 and Level 7 certificates, which are often compared to a Bachelor's degree and a Master's degree respectively. In any case, you leave 42 school with a clearer idea of how the tech job market works, a network of like-minded people, and the understanding that once you have started your journey in the IT world, you can only keep going.❤️
