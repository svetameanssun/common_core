<h1 align="center">42 Programming school common core projects</h1>

If you have never heard about **42 Programming School**, you might wonder what differentiates it from a myriad of other courses you may find online. The answer to this shrewd question might be reflected in one word:

## **THE METHODOLOGY**

(Well, there are technically two words, but they represent one concept.)

The 42 methodology consists of throwing a project at students and having them try to finish it before the deadline.

No teachers, no textbooks.

You can find help (and some emotional support) only among other students: those who have already successfully finished the project or those who have just started it, just like you.
And also those who are in the middle of doing it. Or those who have never done it but have some idea about the technology behind the project... I mean, literally any other student!

You end up learning new, difficult concepts, through trial and error and through constant discussions with like-minded people.

Doesn't it sound like a real-world work experience?
## **What is COMMON CORE?**

The Common Core is a set of projects that helps you to establish a solid technological base to start your journey as a software developer
(or as any other specialist in the IT world, basically).
  
*Feel free to check out my repositories to see some of the Common Core projects I've finished.*

The range of projects goes from writing standard **C library** functions from scratch (strlen, memcmp, calloc, etc.) and recreating your own shell in **C**, to broadening your system administration knowledge using **Docker** and creating your own **IRC** (Internet Relay Chat) server.

You dive into every project understanding that if you do not meet the deadline set for the project, you are out!
  
So, 42 not only gives you the opportunity to work on projects to improve your understanding of the core concepts of programming; it also teaches you to handle high pressure when acquiring new knowledge, helps you improve your time management, and forces you to meet deadlines (the latter three points are the most valuable skills you can get, in my personal opinion).
